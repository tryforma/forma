Population 8,200,000,000. Group chat, 8.

See how small you are. It is the best news you will get today.

Every scoreboard you have ever lost on had the same eight names at the top, and every one of them lives within forty miles of you. Below is the rest of it: the other 8,199,999,992, none of whom have heard of the group chat, most of whom are having a perfectly good Tuesday without your title. Drag it. Let it turn. Find yourself, and then notice how quickly the camera leaves you behind.

Where you are

Somewhere down there, near the fog

There is a dot the size of one pixel on the peninsula. That is you. It has a ring around it so you can find it; nothing else on this ball has ever needed one. Tap a pin, or pick a room below and the globe will take you there.

Someone to adore

For years, in private, you have been practising the one skill this whole site is trying to teach: loving something on purpose, at full volume, with nothing in it for you. That was never the guilty pleasure. That was the muscle.

Company, by the hour

The above-ground version. Tokyo worked out years ago that company is a service like any other, and the rest of the world is catching up on TikTok. None of these is a hostess club. All of them are the kind of thing you would tell the group chat about, if you told the group chat things.

Tokyo · date only

The rental girlfriend

A licensed, above-board service: she meets you at the station, you go to the aquarium or the arcade, she holds the shopping bag and laughs at the right moments, and at six she goes home. About ¥6,000 an hour. Nobody touches anybody, by contract. It exists because a city of fourteen million decided that being accompanied is worth paying for, and they were right.

Shoji Morimoto rents himself out to sit across from you and do nothing. He will come to the restaurant you did not want to eat at alone, the concert with the spare ticket, the courthouse. Room two

Make a call

Having a view about how the world will go, and turning out to be right, is one of the cleaner pleasures available to a person. So call it. Everyone who lands on this page votes on the same questions and the tally is shared, so the moment you pick a side you find out how many strangers picked the other one. Where Kalshi runs the same question we put their price beside ours, because that is the crowd with money on it. The gap between the two is the entertainment.

    Room three

    The company nobody will write about

    The companies in the group chat got written about, which is precisely why you have heard of them; you have spent four years comparing yourself against a shortlist assembled by journalists. The other kind is far more common and almost entirely invisible. One person, a few hundred dollars, and something people pay for on a Tuesday. Nobody will report it, nobody will screenshot it, and it will quietly cover your rent. The whole of it fits in one weekend and one drawer.

    1. Sell the thing people already ask you for.Not the idea in the notes app. The thing three people have come to you about this year already, free, because you are the one who knows how. That was the market research and it came back positive. Write down what you would charge to do it once.
    2. Saturday morning: make it a company.Northwest or Stripe Atlas, a Delaware or Wyoming LLC, about three hundred dollars and the length of one coffee. You will be tempted to spend the rest of the weekend on the name. Give the name one coffee as well, and no more.
    3. Ten minutes later: the EIN.Free, from the IRS, the same afternoon. It is a short form with your own name on it. Everyone skips this one and then sits for three days wondering why the bank has gone quiet.
    4. The bank is a website.Mercury, from the kitchen table, no branch and no minimum. They will want the EIN and a document whose address matches the filing exactly, and the mismatch is the delay almost everybody hits. Then a card turns up with the company’s name on it, and that is the afternoon it stops being a plan.
    5. One page, one sentence, one button.Not a brand. A page that says what the thing is and takes money for it. Vercel is free at your size; Stripe takes a few days to look you over, so start that today and build the page while they read.
    6. The first forty dollars.Message the person who asked. One sentence, one person, before the page is finished. The first payment is small and it rearranges something in you that no promotion ever has. Then find eight more, by name.

    Eight places it actually stops

    None of these is the paperwork. The paperwork is the easy afternoon, which is exactly why people keep doing the paperwork instead of the rest of it.

    1. You will spend a month on the name.And four minutes on what it does. The name is the least consequential line in this entire list and it is the one that will eat your September. Two words on Saturday, then move.
    2. The first message is the whole wall.Not the building, not the page, not the logo. One sentence to one person who already asked you. Send it before you feel ready; ready is not a state that arrives, it is a thing you notice afterwards.
    3. You will price it like an apology.Charge more than is comfortable, and charge monthly. Nine people at forty a month is a company. Ninety people at four is a hobby with a support inbox and a worse life.
    4. The address on the form.The bank compares the document you upload against the filing, character for character. An apartment number in one and not the other is three days of nothing happening while you assume you have been refused.
    5. Stripe will hold the first payout.New accounts get read by a person. Describe the business plainly, put a real support address on it, and expect the first money about a week later. That is the procedure and not a verdict on you.
    6. Week three.The first burst comes from people who like you. Then a fortnight of silence arrives and you conclude the whole thing was a fad. It was not. Week three is where every one-person company is decided, and the decision is simply to send one more message.
    7. The tax nobody set aside.Move thirty percent of every payment into a second account the day it lands, before it feels like yours. Estimates fall due in April, June, September and January. You were not warned because nobody remembers being warned.
    8. Telling the group chat.Not until the ninth customer. Not because it is a secret, and not because they would be unkind. Because the first eight belong to you, and an audience this early turns a business back into a performance.
