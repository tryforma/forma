Population 8,200,000,000. Group chat, 8.

See how small you are. It is the best news you will get today.

Every timeline you have ever measured yourself against was drawn by the same eight women, and every one of them lives within forty miles of you. Below is the rest of it: the other 8,199,999,992, none of whom have seen the ring in the caption, most of whom are having a perfectly good Tuesday without knowing your age. Drag it. Let it turn. Find yourself, and then notice how quickly the camera leaves you behind.

There is a dot the size of one pixel on the east side of the bay. That is you. It has a ring around it so you can find it; nothing else on this ball has ever needed one. Tap a pin, or pick a room below and the globe will take you there.

Company, by the hour

The above-ground version. Tokyo worked out years ago that company is a service like any other, Shanghai turned it into a New Year tradition, and the rest of the world is catching up on TikTok. None of these is a host club. All of them are the kind of thing you would send the group chat, and should.

The rental boyfriend

A licensed, above-board service: he meets you at the station, carries the shopping, takes the photos you actually want taken, and is interested in your day for exactly three hours. About ¥6,000 an hour. Nobody touches anybody, by contract. A city of fourteen million decided being accompanied is worth paying for, and they were right.

    Room three

    You have been running a business for two years

    There is a spreadsheet. It has conditional formatting on it and a tab nobody else has ever opened. There is a group chat with a deeply stupid name that has been going since 2024, a tote in the boot of the car with the good serving spoons in it, and a Venmo request you round down every single time because asking for the whole amount felt rude. Last month eleven people rearranged a Saturday because you sent one message. That is not a hobby, and it is not hosting. It is an operation with excellent logistics and no invoice, and it has been running for two years.

    1. Name the thing you already run.The supper club, the Saturday loop, the ceramics table on Thursdays, the letter you send to forty people on Sundays. It already has a name in your phone. That is the name.
    2. Saturday morning: make it a company.Northwest or Stripe Atlas, a California or Wyoming LLC, about three hundred dollars and the length of one coffee. Forty minutes after that, an entity exists that can hold money and sign for things, which is a strange and good feeling.
    3. Ten minutes later: the EIN.Free, from the IRS, the same afternoon. Everybody skips it and then sits for three days wondering why the bank has gone quiet.
    4. The bank is a website.Mercury, from the kitchen table. The flowers, the twelve kilos of shortrib and the studio hour stop coming out of your own account, and the arithmetic changes the moment they do: it turns out you have been quietly funding this for two years.
    5. Give it a door.Luma or Partiful for the night itself, one page that says what it is, Stripe for the link and Square for the ones who pay at the table. Twenty minutes, and then people can say yes without texting you.
    6. Say the number.Out loud, and without the small laugh after it. This is the hard one and it lasts four seconds. Then it is a business and you never have to do it for the first time again.

    Eight places it actually stops

    Not one of these is the paperwork. The paperwork is a pleasant Saturday, which is why it is the part everyone does first and then stops.

    1. Charging the people who already come.Say the number, then stop talking. The silence lasts four seconds and then it is a business. Anyone who would rather it stayed free was a guest, not a customer, and guests are still allowed; you simply stop counting them as demand.
    2. Food, and the law about food.California’s cottage food permit covers baked goods, jams and the shelf-stable end of things from your own kitchen for a few hundred a year. Anything cooked and plated for a ticketed room wants a commissary kitchen by the hour. Dinner for friends is one thing and a sold-out dinner for strangers is legally another; know which one you are running before the county asks you.
    3. The lease math.Price per seat per hour, multiplied by the seats you can genuinely fill on a Tuesday, not the ones you fill on a Saturday. If the Tuesday number does not cover the rent then it is a Saturday business. Saturday businesses are real and good and should never sign a five-year lease.
    4. Four thousand followers is forty people.That is the honest conversion and it is fine. Put the link where the thumb already is, make paying take one screen, and delete the apology from the caption.
    5. Who am I to.Nobody running a supper club is a chef. Nobody running a run club is fast. Nobody writing the Sunday letter is a journalist. The qualification is that you kept doing it and other people kept turning up, and you have held it for two years.
    6. Week three.The second one sells out and the third one does not, and you decide it was a moment that has passed. It has not. Week three is where every one-person company is decided, and the decision is simply to send one more invitation.
    7. The tax nobody set aside.Move thirty percent of every payment into a second account the day it lands, before it starts feeling like yours. Estimates fall due in April, June, September and January. You were not warned because nobody remembers being warned.
    8. Telling the group chat.Tell them. Then charge them.
