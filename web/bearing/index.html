The thing everyone copied, and the thing nobody did

For most of a century the Boy Scouts ran the best system anyone built for giving a young man a path: seven ranks with a visible top, around 130 merit badges with real requirements, and an adult who watched you do the thing and signed the line. It is falling apart — youth membership dropped 7.4% in a single recent year, on a decade-long slide, through bankruptcy and a rename. Millions of young men now have no ladder at all.

Meanwhile every app on the store has already copied the badges. Streaks, levels, XP, achievements: that is the merit-badge system with the man removed.

But the badge was never the mechanism. The mechanism was that a specific older man knew your name, watched you do it, and signed. The badge was just the excuse for the relationship. Delete the signature and you have a to-do list with confetti, which is why none of them change anybody.

So in Bearing the signature is the product. You can tick your own boxes all day. The app will tell you, plainly, that it earns you nothing.

How it works

  • Pick a markThirty-one of them, across seven fields. Every requirement is written so an adult can look at it and honestly say yes or no.
  • Do the requirementsThree each. Real, boring, specific. Fourteen days without a lie. A hundred hours in one trade. Twenty-five miles on foot.
  • Hand him the phoneNot a text, not a screenshot. He reads what you claimed, he asks you about it, and he types his own name.
  • He can say noAnd should, when it is not true. A mark he would not sign is worth more to you as a no than as a yes.
  • Ranks followMarks accumulate into ranks, with gates: no Load without service, no Frame without a skill someone would pay for.
  • Then you signThe last rank requires that you have witnessed for somebody younger. The ladder is built to make more men who sign.

Who signs — and why there is no matching

Someone who already knows you. An uncle, a coach, a boss, a pastor, an older brother, a neighbour. Someone with enough standing in your life to tell you no.

Bearing has no accounts, no server, no directory, no search, no discovery and no messaging of any kind. There is no mechanism by which this app can put a young man in contact with a stranger, and there never will be. That is not an oversight — Scouting's own catastrophe was abuse, and an app that matched young men with adult strangers would be repeating the worst part of the model while missing the best part.

The witness signs in person, on the young man's own device. The friction is the product and the safeguard at the same time.

Why Proverbs

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”
Proverbs 27:17

That verse is not decoration on this product, it is the architecture. Iron does not sharpen itself. The claim is that the mechanism of a man's formation is friction with another man — which is exactly what the research on at-risk boys keeps landing on, and exactly the part the software industry deleted.

The book of Proverbs is, structurally, a father writing down what he wanted his son to know. For a lot of young men that is precisely the thing that is missing. So it is also the weekly content: twenty-six weeks, King James text, a plain reading with no churchiness, and one specific thing to do that week.

Bearing does not preach and is not affiliated with any church.

Questions

What if I do not have anyone to sign?

Then the first real task is finding one person, and that is harder and more important than any mark on the list. Start with whoever is closest — a boss, a coach, a teacher, a neighbour. Most men say yes when a young man asks them directly, and almost nobody has ever been asked.

Can I just sign my own marks?

Technically nothing stops you typing a name into the box. But then you are a man who lied to a phone, and you will know it. The app is built on the assumption that you would rather it were real.

Is this a Christian app?

The weekly counsel is from Proverbs and the app says so plainly. It does not preach, it is not affiliated with any church, and nothing in the ladder requires you to believe anything.

Is it only for men?

The framing is masculine because that is the gap — the institution that collapsed was the one aimed at boys. Nothing in the ladder stops anyone else using it.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. No account, no server, no analytics. Everything is on your device and gone when you delete the app.
