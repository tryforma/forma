Feeling dimmed has a name

In a survey of 669 people being treated for depression, 46% reported emotional blunting: fewer highs, fewer lows, not crying at the things that should make you cry, not wanting the things you used to want. For many it is a fair trade. For some it is the reason they want to come off one day. Undim exists for the second group, and for the order of operations nobody tells them: build the scaffolding first, then taper, slowly, with a prescriber who is watching.

That order is not a slogan. In the PREVENT trial, people who did an eight-week course first and then tapered with a clinician relapsed no more over two years than people who stayed on maintenance medication.

What it does

  • One thingBehavioural activation: one small act a day, tied to what used to light you up, done whether or not you feel like it. In the COBRA trial it matched CBT. Tiny counts.
  • The flameGrows with your last three weeks and never resets. A missed day dims it a little. There are no streaks to break.
  • Five pillarsMoved, daylight, a person, on purpose, clean inputs. The things every recovery account and every trial keep pointing at. Tap what happened; any size counts.
  • Weekly PHQ-9The nine-question measure clinicians use, public domain, stored only on your phone. Question 9 always shows crisis resources.
  • ReadinessA 0–100 score from your last 28 days: mood steadiness, movement, daylight, people, purpose, a support person, whether your prescriber knows. A conversation starter, not a decision.
  • The reportA one-page plain-text summary worded for a thirty-second read before an appointment, with the questions worth asking, including "can we plan a hyperbolic taper?"

Who it is for

Someone on an SSRI that did its job and now feels like a dimmer switch. Someone in therapy who has run out of things to do between sessions. Someone who typed "how to come off antidepressants" at 2am and found either a forum or a clinic, and wanted something for the year in between. Undim gives them a job for today and a record for the appointment.

Questions

Does Undim tell me to stop my medication?

No, and it never will. Undim does not suggest starting, stopping, skipping or changing a dose. The readiness score is a conversation starter for your prescriber, built from 28 days of your own check-ins. Stopping abruptly is dangerous; a taper is something you plan with the person who prescribes.

Is it therapy?

No. It is a self-care tool built on behavioural activation, and it works alongside therapy or medication rather than replacing either. If you are looking for a therapist, that is a good one thing for today.

What is the readiness score made of?

Mood steadiness over 28 days, days moved, days with daylight, days you reached a person, days on purpose, whether you have named someone in your corner, whether your prescriber knows you want to talk about it, and a PHQ-9 under 10. If the PHQ-9 self-harm question is above zero the score is capped and crisis resources are shown.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. Check-ins, PHQ-9 answers and settings stay on your phone. Only Apple and our subscription provider see purchase status, keyed to a random identifier. Sharing the report is your action, and it never includes your notes or the Clean pillar word.
