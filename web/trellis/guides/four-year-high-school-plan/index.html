The four-year plan, season by season

Holistic admissions is illegible on purpose. This is the syllabus it does not give you: what actually has to happen in each year, and when.

Ninth grade — set the sequence

The main job of ninth grade is not achievement. It is making junior year possible.

  • Map the course sequence backwards. Find the hardest class the school offers in the subject the student likes most, then work back to the ninth-grade prerequisite. The sequence, not the count, is what determines what is available in eleventh grade.
  • Pick two or three activities, not eight. Freshman year is for finding out what holds attention. Three done properly beats eight sampled.
  • Treat first semester as permanent. UC and CSU count tenth and eleventh grade only, but every private college sees the full transcript including ninth.
  • Start a record of hours and roles. Nobody remembers sophomore hours in senior autumn.
  • By spring, line up a real summer. Most competitive programs close between January and April.

Tenth grade — the grades start counting for UC

  • From September, every semester grade is load-bearing for the nine UCs. The capped-weighted GPA is built from tenth and eleventh grade A–G courses only.
  • Sit the PSAT 10 as practice. It does not qualify for National Merit — only the junior-year PSAT/NMSQT does — but a baseline is useful.
  • Ask for a role, do not wait to be offered one. Sophomores get responsibility when they volunteer for one specific job in the autumn.
  • Name the throughline out loud by winter. It can change. Having a working answer directs the next eighteen months.
  • Apply for the junior summer in January and February. This is the most valuable summer available and the deadlines are in winter.

Eleventh grade — the heaviest year

Junior year carries more weight than any other. Early applications are read with junior year as the last complete transcript.

  • Summer before: research, a job, a program, or a project shipped publicly. This block most often becomes the main essay and the top activity line.
  • October: the PSAT/NMSQT. The only sitting that qualifies for National Merit, and California's cutoff is among the highest in the country.
  • Autumn: first SAT or ACT sitting, after a diagnostic of both in the summer. Take the one that scores better; they are not interchangeable.
  • Autumn: take the role with your name on it — officer, captain, editor, founder.
  • Winter: build the first college list, and run the net price calculators before it is fixed.
  • Spring: ask two junior-year teachers for recommendations, in person, before June. Good teachers cap how many they write and fill up early.
  • Spring: draft the personal statement. April in daylight beats October alongside seven supplements.
  • Spring break: visit campuses, including one large public, one small college, and one nobody has considered.

Twelfth grade — execution

  • Before school starts: finish the personal statement. Every essay done in August is an evening back in October.
  • 1 August: the Common App and the UC application both open. Fill in the mechanical sections that week.
  • 1 and 15 November: most Early Decision and Early Action deadlines.
  • 1–30 November: the UC filing window — one application, nine campuses, no early option.
  • Early December: Cal State Apply.
  • Autumn: FAFSA, and the CSS Profile where required. File even if you expect no need-based aid; some merit awards require it.
  • Mid-December: early results. Deferrals are common and are not rejections.
  • 1–15 January: regular deadlines.
  • January–February: the mid-year report goes out. Senior grades still count, and offers are conditional.
  • March–April: compare aid offers line by line — grants, loans and work-study are not the same thing — and appeal in writing where there is a competing offer.
  • 1 May: the national reply date. One deposit, one school, then withdraw the rest.
The two most-skipped items on this page Asking teachers for recommendations before June of junior year, and running the net price calculators before the list is fixed. Both are free, both take an afternoon, and both are extremely painful to fix later.

Common questions

When should you start planning for college?

Ninth grade, but the planning is about course sequence and picking a few activities to do properly — not about applications. The application work itself begins in the spring of junior year.

What is the most important year of high school for college admissions?

Eleventh grade. Early applications are read with junior year as the last complete transcript, and it is also when leadership roles and the highest-value summer are decided.

When should you ask teachers for recommendation letters?

In person, in the spring of junior year, before summer. Teachers cap how many they write and the popular ones fill up early.

When do college applications open?

The Common App and the UC application both open on 1 August. Cal State Apply opens 1 October.
