The PSAT that counts is the one in October of junior year

It is the only sitting that qualifies for National Merit, the cutoff in California is among the highest in the country, and the score it uses is not the number most families look at.

Only eleventh grade counts

The PSAT/NMSQT taken in October of junior year is the sole qualifying test for the National Merit Scholarship Program. A tenth-grade PSAT result — however high — does not qualify for anything. It is practice, and worth taking as practice.

This means there is exactly one attempt, on one morning, with no retake. It is the strongest argument for preparing specifically for this test rather than treating it as a warm-up for the SAT.

The Selection Index, not the score

National Merit does not use the PSAT total score. It uses the Selection Index: the Reading, Writing and Language, and Maths test scores added together and multiplied by two, on a scale of 48 to 228.

The consequence: the verbal side is weighted twice as heavily as maths. Reading and Writing each contribute independently, while maths contributes once. Bay Area families with strong quantitative students routinely under-prepare the exact half of the test that carries the most weight for this particular purpose.

Why California is hard Semifinalist cutoffs are set per state, to distribute roughly 16,000 Semifinalists nationally in proportion to each state’s graduating class. California’s cutoff consistently sits near the top of the national range — several points above the lowest states. The same Selection Index that makes Semifinalist in one state misses in California.

The stages

  1. Commended — roughly the top 50,000 nationally, on a single national cutoff. An honour, but it does not advance.
  2. Semifinalist — roughly 16,000, on the state cutoff. This is the one that matters.
  3. Finalist — most Semifinalists advance, given a confirming SAT or ACT score, a strong record, an essay and a school endorsement.
  4. Scholar — about half of Finalists receive a scholarship, from the corporation, from a sponsoring company, or from a university.

What it is actually worth

The National Merit scholarship itself is modest. The real value is the university-sponsored awards: a number of institutions offer very large, sometimes full-ride packages to National Merit Finalists who name them as first choice. Those schools are mostly not the ones on a typical Bay Area reach list — which is precisely why they are worth knowing about, because they represent one of the few paths to a genuinely free selective education.

At the most selective private colleges, Finalist status is a modest positive in a file, nothing more.

How to prepare, briefly

Sit the sophomore PSAT to get a baseline. Over the junior summer, work the verbal sections specifically — they are two-thirds of the Selection Index. Take timed full tests, in the morning, because that is the condition. And then in October, take it once, and move on.
