A list that will not break your heart

The most common list at a competitive Bay Area high school is fourteen schools, eleven of which admit under fifteen per cent of applicants. That is not a list. It is a lottery ticket in a folder.

The arithmetic nobody does

Applying to nine schools with a seven per cent admit rate does not produce a sixty per cent chance of one acceptance. The outcomes are strongly correlated: the same file goes to all nine, and the readers largely agree with each other about what they are looking at.

A file that reads as exceptional at that level is admitted to several. A file that reads as ordinary at that level — which includes a great many superb students — is admitted to none. There is very little middle.

This is why adding a tenth reach changes almost nothing, and why adding a genuine target changes a great deal.

What a real list looks like

  • Twelve to eighteen schools total. More than that and the supplements get worse, which lowers the odds everywhere.
  • Four or five genuine likelies — and this is the part that gets skipped.
  • A likely school the family would actually be pleased about. A likely nobody in the house respects is not a likely; it is a school the student will refuse to attend in April, which means the list has no floor at all.
The test for a likely Say the sentence out loud at dinner: "She’s going to [school]." If anybody at the table winces, it is not functioning as a likely, whatever the admit rate says.

Bucket by evidence, not by feeling

Look at the published admit rate for the specific major. Engineering and computer science at almost every public university are admitted separately, at rates bearing no relation to the headline number. San José State by major is a completely different school from San José State overall. Cal Poly admits largely by formula, by major.

Then compare the student's numbers against the school's published middle 50% ranges — and be honest that at the most selective schools, being inside those ranges is table stakes rather than an indicator.

Run the net price calculator first

Every American college is legally required to publish one. They take about fifteen minutes with a tax return to hand and are reasonably accurate for straightforward finances.

Run them for every school, together, before the list is fixed. Discovering in April that a school was never affordable is the single most avoidable outcome in this entire process, and it happens to families every single year.

Two engineering salaries in the Bay Area frequently produce an expected family contribution that does not resemble anything the family recognises as achievable, because the formulas do not adjust much for local cost of living. Better to learn that in November of junior year.

Where the merit money actually is

The most selective private colleges give need-based aid only — no merit scholarships at all. Substantial merit money lives one tier down, at the CSUs, at the less-selective UCs, and at private universities actively trying to raise their profile.

A list built entirely from reaches contains no merit money anywhere in it. That is a strategic choice families should make deliberately rather than discover.

The conversation to have in November of junior year

Three questions, answered out loud: What can we afford, without loans, per year? Which two schools on this list would we be genuinely happy about? And what happens in this house if none of the reaches work out?

Answering the third one in advance removes most of the terror from December.

Common questions

How many colleges should you apply to?

Twelve to eighteen is the usual sensible range. Beyond that the supplemental essays get worse, which lowers the odds across the whole list.

How many safety schools do I need?

Four or five genuine likelies, and they must be schools the family would be pleased about. A likely nobody respects does not function as a floor.

Does applying to more reach schools improve my chances?

Barely. Outcomes at highly selective schools are strongly correlated because the same file goes to all of them and readers largely agree. Adding a target changes more than adding a tenth reach.

What is a net price calculator?

A tool every US college is legally required to publish that estimates what your family would actually pay after aid. Run it for every school before fixing the list.
