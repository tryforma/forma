Come, Holy Spirit.

This page is not selling you anything. It has been set aside.

If you came here looking for the apps, they are still here and they are one tap away. Stay a minute first.

An altar · tryforma.app

Who He is ↓
First, the thing most people have wrong

He is not an it.

The Holy Spirit is not a force, a mood, a metaphor for your own courage, or the warm feeling at the end of a good song. He is God: the third Person of the Trinity, as fully a Person as the Father and the Son, with a mind, a will, and a voice.

You can tell from what Scripture says can be done to Him, because none of it can be done to electricity. He can be grieved. He can be lied to. He can be quenched, which is a word you use about a fire someone has deliberately put out. Those are things you do to a Person who is present in the room.

  • The Comforter“I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever.” John 14:16 — another of the same kind as Jesus, and this one does not leave.
  • The Spirit of truth“He will guide you into all truth.” John 16:13 — not into cleverness. Into truth, which is a Person too.
  • The Spirit of adoption“Ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.” Romans 8:15 — He is the reason you get to use that word at all.
  • The earnest of the inheritance“Which is the earnest of our inheritance.” Ephesians 1:14 — a deposit, put down in advance, on everything that is coming.
What He does

Seven things, plainly.

  1. He tells you the truth about yourself, and you survive itHe convicts, which feels terrible for about a day and then feels like fresh air. Conviction is not condemnation: one names a specific thing so you can put it down, the other names you and leaves you nowhere to go. “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.”
  2. He prays when you have no words left“We know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.” If all you can do tonight is sit on the floor, that counts as prayer, and it is being translated.
  3. He guides, and it is usually quietNot a voice over the loudspeaker. A settled certainty that stays after the feeling goes, and keeps agreeing with Scripture no matter how many times you check it against Scripture.
  4. He grows fruit you did not manufactureLove, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. Note that it is fruit, not output. You do not strain for it. You stay attached to the vine and find, months later, that you did not lose your temper in a situation where you always used to.
  5. He hands out gifts, and not to the impressive“Dividing to every man severally as he will.” He gives them as He chooses, to ordinary people, for other people. If you have one, it is not a verdict on your worth; it is a job.
  6. He moves in“Know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you?” Not visits. Dwells. That changes what you do with the body, and it also means you are never alone in a room again.
  7. Where He is, there is room to breathe“Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.” If what you are calling the Spirit is making you frightened, superior, or exhausted, it is worth asking what it actually is.
How to ask

You ask. That is the whole mechanism.

Jesus made it embarrassingly simple, and then made it an argument from the lesser to the greater: “If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?” The comparison is to a bad father who still would not hand his kid a stone. You are not negotiating with someone reluctant.

There is no formula, no posture, no minimum standard of tidiness you have to reach first. If you are waiting to be less of a mess before you ask, you have the order backwards: He is the one who does the cleaning, and He does it from the inside, and He is patient about it.

Holy Spirit, I am asking.

I have been running this on my own strength and I have got exactly this far.

Come into it. All of it, not the tidy part I would show people.

Comfort what is grieving in me. Convict what needs convicting, and be gentle with me while You do it.

Say the words I do not have when I try to pray.

Lead me into the truth, even where the truth is going to cost me something.

Grow the things in me that I have never once managed to grow on my own.

I am not asking for a feeling. I am asking for You.

In the name of Jesus, who promised You would come. Amen.

If you would rather borrow older words, the Church has been praying this one for something like fifteen hundred years: Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Say it out loud. Out loud matters more than you would think, and no, you do not have to feel anything when you finish. Asking is the whole of it. He is not withholding Himself pending your goosebumps.

And then, honestly

He can be grieved, and He will not shout over you.

“Grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.” Grief is what a Person feels when someone they love chooses something that will hurt them. That is the word Paul reached for. Not anger. Grief.

“Quench not the Spirit.” A fire goes out two ways: you pour something on it, or you stop feeding it. Most of us manage it the second way, over months, without ever deciding to. He does not compete for airtime with everything you have on. He waits. The waiting is mercy, not absence, and He is still there when the noise finally stops.

If you have gone quiet on Him for a year, the way back is one sentence long and you already know what it is.

Why He was sent at all

“It is expedient for you that I go away.”

This is one of the strangest things Jesus ever said to people who loved Him: it is better for you that I leave. “For if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.” Better, because a Christ standing beside you can only be in one place, and a Spirit inside you is with all of His people in all of the places at once, including the room you are in now.

But He could only be sent because something had been finished first. Pentecost comes after the cross, not instead of it. Which brings this page to the only part of it that is not about you at all.

It was never yours to settle. Someone who could afford it settled it anyway, in full.

Which leaves the one question still worth asking. What will you do with a life that has already been paid for?

The Spirit is the seal on that receipt. He was given as the proof it was paid.

Ask HimSeven minutes tomorrow morning
Deuteronomy 27

Twelve great stones, plaistered, written on very plainly.

When Israel came over Jordan they were told to do a strange, physical thing before anything else: “Thou shalt set thee up great stones, and plaister them with plaister: And thou shalt write upon them all the words of this law very plainly.” Not a scroll, not a sermon. Stones, whitewashed, standing in the open where everybody walking past has to read them. Joshua did it again at Ebal, and twelve more were carried up out of the riverbed at Gilgal so that when a child asked what they meant, there was something to point at.

So here are ours. Coated white, set upright, written plainly, meant to be read on the way past rather than studied.

  1. Stone 1

    Do not look to the past.

    Isaiah 43:18 · Philippians 3:13
  2. Stone 2

    Do not be given to gluttony.

    Proverbs 23:2 · Philippians 3:19
  3. Stone 3

    Ask, and you shall receive. Knock, and it shall be opened.

    Matthew 7:7 · Luke 11:9
  4. Stone 4

    Whatever you ask in prayer, believe you have received it, and it shall be yours.

    Mark 11:24
  5. Stone 5

    Set aside a tenth of your produce in the third year, so others eat in your town and are satisfied.

    Deuteronomy 14:28–29 · Deuteronomy 26:12
  6. Stone 6

    Do not measure yourself against eight people.

    2 Corinthians 10:12
  7. Stone 7

    Do the thing in front of you, with your might.

    Ecclesiastes 9:10
  8. Stone 8

    Tell the truth, starting with the truth about yourself.

    Ephesians 4:25
  9. Stone 9

    Guard what you look at.

    Job 31:1 · Matthew 6:22
  10. Stone 10

    Forgive quickly. Carry nothing overnight.

    Ephesians 4:26 · Colossians 3:13
  11. Stone 11

    Be not afraid. He has not left.

    Isaiah 41:10 · Hebrews 13:5
  12. Stone 12

    Remember who paid, and live like it.

    1 Corinthians 6:20

More stones as they are given. If one of these is the one you needed today, it is yours; take it off the wall and carry it out.

The altar

Leave what you are carrying. A name, a word, a thing you cannot say anywhere else, a request with no explanation attached. Other people read this wall, and some of them will pray over what you put here. You do not have to sign it.

At the foot, in tribute

Luke 11:13 — “If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?”

Romans 8:26 — “Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.”

Galatians 5:22–23 — “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”
